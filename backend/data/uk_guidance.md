# Trusted UK job-seeking guidance (short)

PathWay must ground employment-info answers in this note. If a question is not covered here, say:
"I'm not sure — please check GOV.UK or ACAS. I cannot give legal or immigration advice."

## CVs in the UK
- Usually 2 pages. Clear headings: personal details, personal statement, key skills, work history (most recent first), education, languages.
- Do not include a photo, date of birth, marital status, or National Insurance number on a standard CV.
- Use UK spelling. Avoid tables and text boxes (they confuse some Applicant Tracking Systems).
- Be honest. Employers can withdraw offers if information is false.
- A career gap can be named briefly (e.g. "family caring responsibilities" or "relocation to the UK") without oversharing.

## Right to work (basics only — not advice)
- Employers must check that a person has the right to work in the UK before they start.
- Documents and processes are on GOV.UK: search "right to work checks".
- This tool cannot say whether someone has the right to work, and cannot complete a visa application.
- Refugees, people with humanitarian protection, and people with Homes for Ukraine sponsorship may have different documents — they should check GOV.UK or a regulated adviser (OISC).

## Interviews
- UK interviews often use competency questions: "Tell me about a time when…" Answer with STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- Arrive a little early. Bring ID if asked. Ask at the end about training and the team.
- It is normal to say "I will need to check childcare" before accepting shifts.
- Discrimination because of race, religion, sex, disability, or pregnancy may be unlawful. ACAS explains this. Citizens Advice can help someone talk it through.

## Pay, hours, and treatment at work
- Most workers are entitled to at least the National Minimum Wage / National Living Wage. Rates change — check GOV.UK.
- Unpaid "trial shifts" that look like real work can be a warning sign. ACAS has guidance.
- An employer should not keep a worker's passport. That can be a sign of exploitation. Contact the Modern Slavery helpline 08000 121 700 or the police on 999 if someone is in danger.
- ACAS helpline: 0300 123 1100. Website: acas.org.uk

## Official signposts (do not invent others)
- GOV.UK — government services and right-to-work information
- National Careers Service — careers information, including for people new to the UK
- ACAS — workplace rights and disputes (not immigration advice)
- Citizens Advice — practical help, including for refugees
- NHS Jobs / NHS Employers — healthcare vacancies and some support-worker routes
- Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) — only official body for UK nurse registration
- Teaching Regulation Agency — QTS / teaching status
- Samaritans 116 123 — if someone is in distress
- 999 — emergency

## Language and confidence
- Many UK employers value reliability, kindness, and teamwork as much as perfect English.
- ESOL (English for Speakers of Other Languages) classes are often free or low-cost via local councils or colleges — search GOV.UK or the local council website.
